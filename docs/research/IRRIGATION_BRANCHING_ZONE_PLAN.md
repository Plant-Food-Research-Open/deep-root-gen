# Irrigation zones and lateral-root branching in DeepRootGen

**Research and implementation plan · 20 November 2026.** Give an active parent root extra opportunities to produce daughters where its own path intersects irrigated soil. A buried drip treatment concentrates those opportunities around the water outlets. A surface treatment favours the wetted part of the upper 0–50 cm. Start with fourth-order parents producing fifth-order daughters in the mature-vine model. A line that never reaches eligible parent material cannot create a new root there.

The design below was frozen before the study. The [generator inputs and runnable example](IRRIGATION_INPUTS.md) document the implementation.

## 1. What the experiments support

| Primary evidence | Observation | Design consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Myburgh, Lower Orange River vineyard trial, figures 5–6 | Profile-wall maps recorded fine-root intersections per square metre for furrow, surface drip and subsurface treatments. Concentration around the lines was more apparent at 30 and 45 cm burial than at 15 cm. The author also discusses a possible herbicide effect. [Field maps and treatments, printed p. 37](https://news.sawine.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/2007_2008-Technical-Yearbook-WEB.pdf) | Locate the zone in three dimensions relative to the actual outlet. The installation depths provide useful scenario checks. Site soil, management and wetting extent determine its dimensions. |
| Soar & Loveys (2007), grapevines converted from diffuse irrigation to drip | Root volume increased beneath the drip line, especially at 25–50 cm. The largest RLD increase occurred in the 1–4 mm diameter classes. Soil texture also influenced the depth distribution. [Field study](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1755-0238.2007.tb00066.x) | Use a shallow wetted strip for surface drip. Compare the upper and lower halves of the 0–50 cm layer. A second, prespecified scenario may give the lower half more weight. The reported diameter class overlaps Calvin’s observation band only partly. |
| Ma, Jacoby & Sanguinet (2020), Cabernet Sauvignon | Compared with surface drip, delivery through tubes to 60 cm gave 30–40% lower RLD and 50–60% fewer observed roots in the top 60 cm under high and moderate irrigation. At the low rate the shallow treatment difference was not significant. [Field design and results](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpls.2020.575303) | Allow a neutral response and a treatment-dependent depth profile. The tubes constitute a separate deep-delivery system. Keep 60 cm as a documented comparison case. |
| Bao et al. (2014), experiments in Arabidopsis and cereals | Contact with available water influenced the side of a parent root on which laterals formed. [Primary experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24927545/) | Supports the plausibility of a local branching response. Orientation on a seedling root and daughter births per centimetre on a mature grapevine are different measurements. |

The vineyard studies constrain **where root material occurs**. More length in a core could arise from more daughters, longer axes, changed diameter or longer survival. No study above estimates the number of extra fifth-order daughters per eligible centimetre on Calvin’s vines. The multiplier below is a hypothesis about that mechanism. Record irrigation rate, years under the treatment and soil texture as context. A low-rate or recently converted site may show little response.

## 2. Describe the wetted soil independently of the roots

Use the model soil frame in centimetres. Let x be across the row, y along the row and d positive depth below the surveyed soil surface. Transform field positions once. Rootstock burial and irrigation outlet depth are separate measurements. For each parent position define an exposure K(x,y,d) from 0 (outside the wetted footprint) to 1 (maximum preference).

| Treatment | Proposed exposure | Inputs and starting scenarios |
|---|---|---|
| Buried drip | A smooth ellipsoid around each outlet. Closely spaced emitters with overlapping wetting may instead use a continuous strip along the measured line. | Supply line offset, outlet depth, emitter spacing and along-row origin for discrete outlets. Measure horizontal and vertical wetting extents. If the installation is unknown, compare 15, 30 and 45 cm as labelled literature scenarios. Use 30 cm for the illustrative first screen. |
| Surface irrigation over the whole sampled area | Full horizontal coverage within the wetted plot. Depth weight is 1 from the surface through 40 cm and falls smoothly to 0 at 50 cm. | Use this treatment for documented broad coverage. The owner’s 0–50 cm band defines the test. The 10 cm transition is a modelling choice. |
| Surface drip or a wetted strip | Multiply the same depth weight by a horizontal footprint around the measured row line, individual emitters or wetted furrow. | Give dry inter-row soil zero supplemental weight. Where field evidence supports a subsurface maximum, compare a depth profile peaking at 37.5 cm and confined to 0–50 cm. |

For a concrete implementation use the raised cosine C(r) = (1 + cos(πr))/2 for 0 ≤ r < 1 and zero for r ≥ 1. For a buried outlet, r² = (Δx/a)² + (Δy/b)² + (Δd/c)² about the wetting centre. The radii a, b and c are distances from the centre to the edge, in cm. If wetting extends farther below the outlet, permit independently observed upper and lower vertical radii. Keep the centre at the outlet unless a moisture transect supports an offset. A strip uses perpendicular horizontal distance and depth. Combine overlapping outlets with the maximum K, clip at the soil surface, and repeat only the measured vineyard layout. For surface irrigation use C((d−40)/10) between 40 and 50 cm. For the optional deeper-peaked profile use C(|d−37.5|/37.5) above the peak and C((d−37.5)/12.5) below it. All these depth rules use zero supplemental weight below 50 cm for the surface treatment.

Prefer repeated soil-moisture transects or mapped wetted boundaries during representative irrigation cycles. Where only nominal dimensions exist, document the assumptions and test narrow and broad footprints separately. Illustrative buried-bulb radii of 30 cm across-row, 30 cm along-row and 20 cm vertically allow software tests before field dimensions are available. They are not estimates for Site 1. Keep outlet positions fixed when comparing architecture parameters. Surveyed irrigation features stay fixed in the vineyard frame even when the vine is rotated or translated.

For example, consider a parent directly under the drip line, with a buried outlet at 30 cm and a vertical wetting radius of 20 cm. The two treatments predict different depths of maximum daughter formation. The numbers below give the **relative local rate for movable sites** at peak multiplier 2, calculated as 1 + K. An active, eligible parent still has to cross the indicated depth.

| Depth below surface | Buried drip | Surface treatment, peak throughout 0–40 cm |
|---|---:|---:|
| 5 cm | 1.00× | 2.00× |
| 20 cm | 1.50× | 2.00× |
| 30 cm | 2.00× | 2.00× |
| 40 cm | 1.50× | 2.00× |
| 45 cm | 1.15× | 1.50× |

## 3. Generate genuine additional daughters

Use the base law’s parent activation and usable contour. Preserve each existing proposed attachment. Add an independent draw of supplemental sites on active parents that encounter the wetted soil. Specify a **peak multiplier m**, initially 1, 1.25, 1.5 or 2. The value 1 is the exact control. At 2, each fully exposed eligible centimetre receives an extra expected proposal rate equal to its background rate. Outside the footprint the extra rate is zero.

Let s be distance along the final parent centreline. Let E be the part of the usable parent contour where local parent diameter is **at least 0.093 cm (0.93 mm)**. Compute exposure-weighted eligible length H in cm and draw supplemental daughters as follows.

- H = ∫ over E of K(x(s),y(s),d(s)) ds. Evaluate true contour length through the zone. This makes the daughter response independent of how many numerical steps or export vertices happen to lie inside it.
- For the existing Poisson spacing law, the background rate λ₀ is 1 / mean spacing in cm. Use its current basal and apical exclusions. An activated parent whose background Poisson draw happened to be zero can still draw extras. A parent rejected by the activation draw cannot.
- Calvin’s selected order-4 law draws a first site s₁ and a conditional count N from its existing distribution. Keep that first site and all baseline draws. For this law use λ₀ = (N−1)/(L−s₁) on the distal interval, where L is parent length. This is the sampled background density of additional sites after the first. A rejected activation, an out-of-range first site, N = 1, or an empty interval gives zero supplemental sites under this version. Record these cases in diagnostics. This choice preserves the first-site interpretation and avoids converting Calvin to a different count law for the experiment.
- Draw Nextra from Poisson((m−1) λ₀ H). When positive, draw its positions on E with density proportional to K per centimetre. Sort, apply the shared coincident-site and diameter checks, and allocate each accepted daughter through the normal generator. Daughters inherit their configured order-specific length, taper, cap and direction laws. The peak multiplier controls an expected local proposal rate. Individual parents and realized daughter counts vary.
- Numerical example under the spacing law: a parent has 100 cm of eligible contour and mean spacing 10 cm. A 20 cm section is fully wetted, with zero weight elsewhere. At m = 2 the expected count rises from 10 to 12. Four daughters are expected in the wet section and eight elsewhere. At m = 1.5 the total expectation is 11. These values are conditional on activation, before any coincident-site handling.

A **redistribution-only comparison** keeps the same realized proposal count while weighting eligible positions by 1 + (m−1)K. Preserve the anchored first site, parent activation, and any baseline ineligible proposals for this comparison. Report changes in accepted counts as a check. This is an experimental comparator for the question “does moving daughters suffice?” The proposed irrigation treatment itself permits an increase. A uniform-increase control can also distribute the same realized number of extras across all eligible contour on each matched parent. That comparison isolates the benefit of placing the extras near irrigation.

Expose only fields used by the chosen footprint. Validate finite lengths, positive radii/spacing, bounded layer depths, registered coordinates, m ≥ 1, compatible branch laws and the maximum order. 3. **Make final parent geometry available at the decision.** The present engine calls the branch-site law while subdividing a *local* polyline. Vineyard placement and curvature are realized later. Sampling the irrigation field at those provisional coordinates would give a response to the wrong location. For the terminal-order pilot, stage base construction through the realized parent curve; reserve supplemental attachments before final materialization. Draw the additional daughters through the same birth, geometry and validation routines as the baseline daughters. If this requires splitting a lifecycle operation, keep one shared parent-first path. 4. **Integrate and sample consistently.** Clip each piecewise-linear parent interval to the diameter-eligible contour and the wetting support. Subdivide at footprint boundaries, including narrow bulbs lying entirely between two outside vertices. Integrate the smooth field to a documented tolerance and invert the cumulative integral for sampling. Use the final attachment coordinate once for both field evaluation and receipt. Apply the same threshold convention at equality as the ordinary gate. Give extra proposals stable identities and separate random streams so enabling a terminal daughter cannot reroll the rest of the vine. Reserve the aggregate proposal count before allocating nodes. Reuse the common material and resource checks. [Background Poisson implementation](../../deeprootgen/model/branching_laws.py) · [First-site/count implementation](../../deeprootgen/model/laws.py)
5. **Handle the vineyard frame in every caller.** The selected Site 1 analysis uses neighbouring placements and four reflected orientations. Reusing a pre-grown vine at a new position is exact for this feature only when that transform also preserves the irrigation field. Otherwise, reuse the parent scaffold if valid, transform it to the new placement, then evaluate exposure and generate its affected daughters there. Record distinct transformed cases and their actual computational cost. The same requirement applies to an off-centre or one-sided drip line.
6. **Record what changed.** For each affected order and parent, save activation, available contour, diameter-eligible length, H, background intensity, expected and drawn extras, accepted baseline/extra counts, and rejection reasons. Report realized birth depth, distance to line and daughters per metre of eligible parent, by exposure band. Summarize root counts and length by order, and dry mass and length on each side of the 2.5 mm reporting split. Preserve subject-only biomass and per-placement core contributions.

## 5. Verify the rule before testing a vineyard

| Check | Expected outcome |
|---|---|
| Neutral, inactive or out of zone | Disabled and m = 1 reproduce seeded baseline geometry, counts and observation outputs exactly. Active metadata can differ. Zero exposure gives zero extras. Parent order 5 cannot create order 6 when the maximum order is 5. |
| Eligible contour | Test a straight parent crossing the boundary, a curved parent passing through twice, an entirely dry parent, and taper crossing 0.93 mm inside the zone. Check equality, empty intervals, near-tip sites and capping. Accepted extras lie on eligible parent material at the recorded birth coordinates. |
| Analytic count and placement | On the 100 cm example above, verify the integral and expected count deterministically. On a fixed panel of cheap synthetic parent draws, compare the count mean, variance and zone fraction with their analytic sampling intervals. Check both base laws, including the anchored first site, stratified distal sampling if active, and a zero background Poisson draw. |
| Spatial geometry | For surface treatment check depths 0, 25, 40, 45, 50 and 60 cm. For buried treatment check the centre, edge, translation and overlap of outlets. A narrow bulb crossed by one 2 cm interval must be detected. Subdividing the identical polyline must preserve integrated exposure and its position distribution to numerical tolerance. |
| Generator integration | A paired-seed run leaves upstream parent geometry and baseline daughters traceable. Verify exact quota conflicts, proposal limits, zero-material support nodes, topology, valid lengths, baseline and extra mass reconciliation, and CLI/notebook export/reload. A reflected vine near a one-sided line must respond on the surveyed side. |

## 6. Test whether the feature helps Calvin Site 1

**Site evidence first.** Assemble irrigation method at the sampling date and over the vine’s lifetime, depths and offsets of outlets, row and vine spacing, approximate wetted width, and core locations in the same field frame. Note irrigation dose/frequency, rainfall and any restrictive layer information available. With an unknown layout, publish separate surface and buried *scenarios*. Locating emitters by maximizing agreement with the same root cores would make the comparison circular. Mapping soil impedance or root turning at a layer belongs to a separate hypothesis.

| Stage | Candidate set and comparison | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| A. Exposure audit | On archived or regenerated parent curves at all relevant placements, plot eligible order-4 length and H by depth and distance to the irrigation source. Overlay core supports and measured wetting if available. | If a zone contains negligible eligible parent length, more daughters there cannot materially change this reconstruction. Record that finding before fitting larger multipliers. |
| B. Paired screen | Freeze a common panel of eight development seeds. Start with the neutral reference and m = 1.25, 1.5 and 2 for each of two specified footprints: one buried and one surface. Compare seven settings on eight seeds, or 56 candidate-by-seed evaluations before reuse. For confirmed surface drip use a strip; for broad surface wetting use full coverage. | Within each treatment, compare placement and encounter frequency as well as RLD. Record emitted geometries, reused parents, neighbour evaluations and full generator calls separately. |
| C. Discriminate mechanisms | For the leading strength of each plausible treatment, compare the additive wet-zone rule with redistribution alone and with equal numbers of extras placed uniformly. Keep whole-vine counts, eligible contour and realized births available for interpretation. As separate sensitivities, vary wetting width by ±25%, compare the 25–50 cm emphasis for surface irrigation or vary burial depth within survey uncertainty. Cross the winning irrigation treatment with positional taper on/off using both matched neutral references. | A gain confined to one unverified geometry, one extreme core, or a change in diameter classification is weak evidence for extra branching. Document trade-offs. |
| D. Confirmation | Lock the candidate, footprint, folds and primary criteria before a fresh panel of at least 16 seeds. Compare against the neutral reference, depth-only benchmark and best equal-count comparator using the same core-depth cells. Report paired uncertainty by simulation seed and sensitivity to leaving out each complete development core. | Report the three previously inspected comparison cores as a historical comparison. Strong evidence of transfer requires new cores or vines, preferably across treatment, emitter distance and depth. |

**Primary endpoint.** Keep the existing grouped-core evaluation: 50% RLD magnitude error, 25% joint core-by-depth total variation and 25% depth-profile total variation. Retain the observed core volumes, physical core positions, documented QC and 0.93–2.5 mm observation band. Give the established 7.6 cm virtual-core and modest overprediction preference their own sensitivity results. A dense patch that improves the mean can still increase misses elsewhere. Report core-weighted zero encounters, RLD conditional on a hit, physical mean predicted/observed ratio, and profiles by emitter distance and depth. For the 25 and 50 cm boundaries, preserve the original sampling increments in the score; flag straddling increments or aggregate to compatible bins for descriptive plots. Baseline, observations and candidates must use the same support. [Architecture scoring](../ARCHITECTURE_SCORING.md)

**Promotion criterion.** Prespecify a within-treatment winner on development cores. On confirmation require lower physical joint core-by-depth error and lower composite loss than the neutral reference, check the direction against the equal-extra-count comparator and report the depth-only benchmark. Retain the existing mean RLD ratio guard (0.8–1.2) and mean subject underground dry-mass guard (5–7 kg, same crown convention and density within a comparison). Report the fraction of individual vines within the mass band and the 0.30 loss target explicitly. If uncertainty spans no improvement, retain the rule as exploratory. A strong spatial fit can support its predictive value. To estimate the actual daughter response, measure attachment counts per metre of eligible parent inside and outside the wetted zone, alongside root diameter and soil moisture.

**Outputs for review.** Deliver the schema proposal and validated synthetic treatment examples, code and test receipts, a local notebook containing frozen settings and all trials, maps of soil exposure and daughter births, matched core-depth plots, and a table of gains and errors for each treatment. The notebook should distinguish assumed and measured irrigation dimensions and link each result to its exact seed, layout and model configuration.
